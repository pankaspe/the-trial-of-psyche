# The Trial of Psyche - level II.4: "The Temple of Ceres and Juno"
# The last of Act II: one temple at dusk, a real puzzle that looks easy. The
# cella is a sacred pool; over it the floor is a mechanism of two square rings
# about the altar, each turning a quarter at a time (handles), with fixed
# pillars at their corners:
#   - the outer ring (h2) carries two flights up to the middle ring; its
#     handle is on the middle ring's north-west pillar (inside);
#   - the middle ring (h3) has a balcony open toward the altar (h4), no
#     flight: seen from the right side it touches the altar (an illusion).
#     From the porch it is right there, but cut off on both sides. Its two
#     handles are on the porch and on the outer ring's south-east pillar;
#   - two of the middle ring's corner pillars stand; the others rise from the
#     pool when the lamp is lit on the seals of Juno's altar (east: the
#     north-east pillar) and of Ceres' (west: the south-east one).
# A stone of the outer ring is veiled (the way to Juno, at the start). The
# altar hides the flights behind it from some views: turn, or climb them in
# the light. No dead ends: every turn can be undone.
# Solution (solver: 101 steps, 1 turn, 3 lightings, 11 handles): Juno's seal;
# the middle ring three times from the outer ring's pillar; the outer ring
# once from the middle ring's pillar; the middle ring once from the porch;
# Ceres' seal; the outer ring twice; the middle ring once from the outer
# pillar, three times from the porch; the balcony, a turn, the altar.
# Fragments, each on a pedestal in the pool reached by an illusion, the rings
# turned for it (four or five handles each): II.4 (V.29-31) and II.4c (V.31)
# between the outer and the middle ring, south-west and north-west; II.4b
# (VI.2) behind Ceres, view 3 from the outer ring's north-west pillar; II.4d
# (VI.4) behind Juno, at the back.
